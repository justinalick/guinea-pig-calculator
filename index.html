Herd size

The average of all the ways the herd could go, on a log scale. Starting pigs are mature and unrelated, and the sows are bred on day 0. Daughters first give birth at age M + gestation, then every interval until they die or stop breeding. Births need a fertile boar, floor space (if limited) and are reduced by inbreeding.     How the model works

    One trip around the loop is a generation: a sow's daughters grow up and become breeding sows themselves. Every arrow can lose pigs, and the numbers follow your assumptions.

    Source and caveats. This is not an official mortality table. It comes from one study (Mata, PeerJ 2025): Kaplan–Meier on the ages at death of 675 pet guinea pigs that died under UK vet care in 2019 (Royal Veterinary College VetCompass records), in half-year intervals with no censoring.

    • It is the age-at-death distribution of one year's deaths, not a cohort followed from birth. Treating it as death probabilities by age assumes the pet population was stable over time, which is untested.
    • Only animals that reached a vet are included. Pups that die at breeders or before sale (~6–8 weeks) are mostly missing, so early-age deaths are likely understated. Pup losses are modelled separately through s.
    • These are pets, not breeding sows. Sows that breed repeatedly likely die faster at the same age; d and hb cover that, but hb is an assumption with no data behind it.
    • The tail rests on very few animals: the 7.5–8 yr value (0.27) is smoothed to 0.46, and values past 8.5 years are extrapolated.

    ρ1 ≈ 0.8: first litters averaged 3.2–3.5 pups vs 3.6–4.7 for later ones in a breeding colony (Rowlands, J. Hygiene, 1949). φ: pups of sows over 900 days survived 0.43 vs 0.71 in a lab colony (ratio 0.61).

    Daughters per sow (R₀) and growth rate (Euler–Lotka)

    This counts litters by number (j), which is exact for an uncrowded herd with a boar present. The simulation tracks, for each age group, the share of sows that haven't had a litter yet, so a sow that misses a cycle (crowding, no boar) still gets the smaller first litter when she finally breeds.

    The herd grows if and only if R₀ > 1; the right side falls as r rises, so there is exactly one root. T̄ is the generation time (average age of mothers). This r is the growth rate of an uncrowded, non-inbred herd; crowding and inbreeding only slow it.

    Boar availability

    The chance a fertile boar was alive at conception: all nb starting boars dead, and no young fertile male (Poisson approximation, Mb = expected males born on day b). β → 1 as the herd grows.

    Crowding (no selling or neutering)

    A sow conceives with probability δ at conception time; a sow that doesn't waits for her next cycle. With no culling, births fall to zero as the herd fills its space, so it levels off near K. A fast-growing herd can overshoot K for a while (by ~15% with no deaths and back-to-back litters) because pregnancies already underway still deliver. If N > K, the herd never reaches N. This logistic form is an assumption: guinea pigs breed less when crowded, but the exact curve isn't measured.

    Inbreeding

    Nf = sows of breeding age, Nm = fertile boars, θ = average relatedness (coancestry) of the breeders, F = inbreeding of pups born now. Starting pigs are assumed unrelated. 2B is the number of lethal equivalents: about 3.1 is the median for juvenile survival across 40 zoo mammal populations (Ralls, Ballou & Templeton, Conservation Biology 1988; the exact figure couldn't be checked against the paper). At F = 0.25 (full-sib parents) that cuts pup survival by about 32%. This uses effective population size, not a full pedigree; the Monte Carlo uses the same approximation with actual counts. In the average model Ne comes from average herd sizes, which runs that died out pull down, so for very small starting herds (under ~10) it overstates inbreeding and runs a few percent to ~15% pessimistic; the Monte Carlo is the better guide there.

    Whole-litter stillbirths

    Same average, more variable outcomes; it only matters in the Monte Carlo.

    Costs and space

    Upkeep is summed over pig-days until the herd reaches N. Default prices are pet-owner prices; a breeder buying in bulk would pay less per pig.
